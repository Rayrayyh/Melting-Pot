# Section two names the problem and opens three doors

Chosen 2026-08-31 by the owner after a brutal audit of the landing's second section.

Before this, both hero CTAs anchored to a class-code form. A first-time visitor almost never has a code, so the page's two highest-intent clicks ended at a disabled button, the teacher path was a 13px footnote under a card labelled "For students", the seeded demo class was mentioned nowhere, and "See how the melt works" was dead text. A panel of three critics (cold visitor, UX heuristics, landing precedent) reached the same verdict independently: a checkout aisle at the store entrance.

Now the section opens on the problem in the owner's approved headline, "The part you need is in someone else's handwriting", with the eyebrow "How notes reach the class" and a professional subheadline that walks from a classmate's notebook to what the organizer does and who approves. Under it, three equal doors: open the live demo Pot (HXU863) without an account, enter a class code (button never disabled, a short code gets a plain sentence and no request), or create a Pot. The hero, header and closing-band CTAs all land on #join; #spaces survives for stale links; the melt hint is a real link.

Copy rules that shaped it: no invented numbers, no mirrored two-sentence constructions, no slogans, nothing casual. The rejected drafts (a "Thirty classmates" mirror, "Skip the group chat", "shaky bits") are recorded here so nobody drifts back to them.

Risks accepted: the demo code is public on the page, so anyone can join during judging; the owner can regenerate it in Pot settings afterwards. The hero CTA row also moved from the 1.5x gap to the 1.7x mid band gap (mt-8), the proximity sweet spot the owner picked from four measured presets.
